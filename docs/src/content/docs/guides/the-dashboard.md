---
title: The dashboard
description: Where kilna opens — what needs a decision, what goes out this week, and what is closest to being worth releasing.
---

The dashboard is the first screen, and it asks one question: **what needs you
right now?** Not what exists — the [catalogue](/kilna/guides/the-catalogue/)
answers that — but what is waiting.

Nothing on this screen changes anything. Every row is a way into somewhere
else.

The screen is two columns, each scrolling on its own. In the side column —
the catalogue in figures, what is running, what the workspace noticed, your
own lines, what happened since you last looked — every list scrolls inside its
own panel: a short one stands whole, a long one takes the height that is left.
Forty findings no longer push the figures off the top. The column itself
scrolls only on a window too low to show a few lines of each.

## Needs a decision

Releases whose date is close and whose work is not ready for it: whatever the
place's [checklist](/kilna/guides/planning-a-release/#ready-marks) still
misses - a version, a score, a cover, a field nobody wrote. Each row says what
is lacking in words, carries the same readiness marks the
[calendar](/kilna/guides/planning-a-release/) uses, and its button opens the
work on the tab that closes the first gap: the score, the versions, the cover,
or the overview where the release's fields are written.

Overdue slots sort to the top and are drawn in red. A date that has passed with
the work still unfinished is the loudest thing kilna has to say.

A gap further off than a week is not listed here. It is real, but it is not a
decision yet, and a dashboard that lists everything eventually gets ignored.

## Next 7 days

Everything holding a slot inside the week, soonest first, ready or not — the
week as it actually stands. A release that is ready appears here with a tick
and nothing else to do; that is the point of seeing it.

## Closest to release

Work that has been scored and is going nowhere: nothing booked, nothing out.
The strongest first, because that is the order you would pick them up in.
This is the shortlist of what to work on next, not the whole catalogue — six at
most.

## Nothing has judged these yet

Works with no score at all, listed as names. Nothing can rank them until
something does, so they cannot appear anywhere else on this screen. Clicking one
opens it on the Score tab.

Already-released work is left out of every section, including this one. The
rule holds across kilna: **the automation shows what can still be done**, never
what is finished.

## Worth doing

Below the sections above, the **focus board**: standing complaints the workspace
noticed about itself, and lines you put there yourself. The two look alike and
behave differently, and the difference is worth knowing.

### What the workspace noticed

Things that are true today and were true last week. A score that describes a
draft you have since rewritten. The weakest work holding a slot while something
stronger waits. A draft nobody has opened in a month. And, since v0.97, the
day after a release: one whose day has passed and that is still not marked
out, and one out for two days with no link kept for it - see
[after the day](/kilna/guides/planning-a-release/#marking-released). Since
v0.104, a month that holds fewer releases than the
[rhythm](/kilna/guides/planning-a-release/#the-rhythm-and-the-auto-layout)
makes room for, with how many wait in the queue; pressing it opens the
calendar on the month. And since v0.105, one of [your own lines](#your-own-lines)
whose day has come.

**Nothing here changes anything.** A finding names a work and what is off about
it; clicking the text opens the work at the tab that answers it. Where a profile
action fits the complaint, it is offered as a button — pressing it starts that
action in a chat of its own, exactly as it would from the work's own card. The
answer arrives as a proposal you apply or ignore. The assistant never writes to
your workspace.

Findings the sections above already show are left out rather than said twice.

A finding says nothing about a slot you settled by hand, or about work that has
gone out or a date that has passed - except to ask for the mark or the link
the record still lacks - the same rule the catalogue's gap filters follow:
**the automation shows what can still be done**. And the weak-slot complaint
stays quiet unless there is genuinely something stronger to put there; telling
you a work is weak with no alternative is a remark, not a finding.

A finding has no pin and no place in an order you set. It does not need one: it
appears when its complaint becomes true and leaves the moment it stops being
true, without anything having to remember it.

### Heard it

The **×** on a finding puts that complaint away. What is remembered is the
complaint itself, not the work and not the kind — so the same complaint stays
quiet, and a changed one is news again.

That distinction is the whole point. A draft you dismissed after a month of
silence comes back when it has sat four; a work you told kilna you had heard
about its missing score says nothing more about the score, but still speaks up
when its slot turns out to be the weak one. Hiding is answering *this*, not
silencing the work.

Everything put away is behind a count at the bottom of the board — `3 put away`
— which opens into the list. Clicking one shows it again. Nothing is hidden
permanently and nothing is hidden by accident beyond recovery.

Complaints about a work you later delete are forgotten the next time kilna
starts.

### Your own lines

**Add a line** puts a note on the board: something to keep in front of you that
kilna would never notice on its own — a question for a collaborator, an artwork
you are waiting on, a decision you want to sleep on.

A note is yours, so it behaves the opposite way to a finding. It stays until you
rub it out. You can drag it by its handle to put it where you want, and pin it
to keep it at the top. The automation never removes one and never reorders one.

The **⋯** of a line gives it a day - today, tomorrow, in a week, or none - and
ties it to a work, or unties it. A line tied to a work is the way to it: its
words open the work's card. A line with a day shows the day beside it, in the
warning colour once it has come; and on its day:

- it stands under **Today**, at the top of the board - at most three lines,
  the oldest day first: the day's plan, not a second list;
- it is said among the findings - *"Ask the label" - its day was Oct 27* -
  until it is rubbed out or given another day.

## Since your last visit

The column's last widget lists what happened since you last marked the
[history](/kilna/guides/the-history/) seen, newest first, and above it one
line counts it: the events still unread, and the assistant's runs that ended
meanwhile - and how many of them failed - which the history does not list
one by one.

## When it is quiet

A workspace where everything is scored, booked or shipped — and where nothing
was found worth doing — says so in a single line. That is the screen working,
not failing: there is genuinely nothing waiting on you, and inventing something
to show would be worse than saying so.

One standing finding is enough to keep the screen busy. A scored, booked work
whose draft moved after the score appears in no section above, and claiming
nothing is waiting while that stands would be a confident lie.

The board stays on a quiet morning even when the sections are gone: your own
lines and the way back to what you put away are the parts of this screen that
are yours, and a quiet day is exactly when they are worth reading.
