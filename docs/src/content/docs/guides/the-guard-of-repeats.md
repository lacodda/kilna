---
title: The guard of repeats
description: A song held against the songs that already went out before it goes out itself - orange for a spent word, red for a rare word the audience heard lately - wherever the song is seen.
---

The [register](/kilna/guides/the-register/) says what your body of work has
spent while you write. The **guard** says it once more at the step that
counts: before a song goes out. A song that has not gone out - nothing made
from it has - is held against every song that went out and every song that
holds a day in the calendar, and what they share is a **repeat**:

- **Orange - said before.** A term of the register both songs say (*кофе*,
  *шторы*, *холодильник*): spent, and one more use says nothing new, however
  long ago the other song went out. Or a rare word the other song said long
  ago.
- **Red - said lately.** A rare word the other song said within the last 90
  days - or will say, when it is booked within them: a listener who heard
  *пульсар* last month hears it again.

A word is **rare** when you keep it in the
[bank of words](/kilna/guides/the-bank-of-words/), or when it is a Russian
word both far down the language's frequency list (past the 20 000th stem) and
in no more than two of your own works - a word you say in twenty songs is
yours, and spent only if the register says so. No model is asked; the
frequency list ships with kilna. A word in another script is rare only when
the bank keeps it.

Nothing is stored. The repeats are read off the texts and the calendar each
time you look: rewrite the line and the repeat goes; move the other song's
release out of the window and red turns orange.

## Where you see it

The mark - a dot in the hue of the loudest repeat, with how many when there
are several - stands wherever the song or what is made from it stands: the
catalogue, the calendar's chips and its queue, the dashboard's week, the
header of the song and of its clip, its audio and its shorts. Hover it:
*пульсар — in “Tide”, out Sep 20*.

On the card's overview, **Repeats** lists them all, loudest first - one row a
word, however many terms of the register catch it and however many songs say
it: the word, whether it is a spent term or a rare word, the song that makes
it loudest (one click away) with its day, and how many more songs say it.

## I know, keep it

A repeat can be on purpose - a callback, a refrain across an album. **I
know, keep it** leaves the word marked in the list and stops it counting
toward the song's mark; **Count it again** takes that back. The decision is
about this song and this word only, it is in the history, and undo takes it
back. When every repeat of a song is kept, the song wears no mark.

## On the calendar

Dragging a publication onto a day, or picking its date, says when that day
would put it within the window of a song it shares a rare word with: *Too
close: пульсар — in “Tide”, out Sep 20*. It is a warning, not a refusal - the
release lands where you put it.

## Meanings

The guard catches words. An image told in other words - *a lighthouse nobody
keeps* - is the assistant's to catch: Studio's **Meanings** action reads a
song against the songs that went out and proposes the images and scenes they
share as links in the register. Once kept, a meaning named for both songs is
an orange repeat like any spent term. See
[The register](/kilna/guides/the-register/#meaning-and-the-assistant).

## Your numbers

**Settings → Profile → Guard of repeats** sets the window (90 days), the rank
a word is rare from (20 000) and how many of your works a rare word may stand
in (2). See `guard` in [the profile document](/kilna/reference/profile-document/#guard).
Why it is built this way:
[ADR 0054](https://github.com/lacodda/kilna/blob/main/docs/adr/0054-the-guard-of-repeats-is-derived-and-a-kept-word-is-stored.md).
